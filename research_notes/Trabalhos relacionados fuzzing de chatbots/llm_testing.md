# Fuzzing and automated testing of LLMs and chatbots (Related Work notes)

Verification method: every entry below was checked on 2026-10-28 against the arXiv API record (export.arxiv.org, `id_list=` query returning title, authors, date, abstract, author comments, journal_ref and DOI) and, where a DOI is given, against the Crossref API record (api.crossref.org/works/DOI returning title, venue, year and authors). Venue information marked "(author comment)" comes from the arXiv comment field and was not independently cross-checked against the publisher. No entry was added from memory alone.

Mapping to our failure categories: [ARITH] arithmetic, [COUNT] letter counting, [REV] string reversal, [FMT] JSON/format violations, [ECHO] verbatim Unicode echo, [RUNTIME] API/runtime errors.

## 1. Fuzzing LLMs and LLM applications; surveys of software testing with/of LLMs

### Takeaway
Existing LLM fuzzers (GPTFuzzer, FuzzLLM, PromptFuzz, HouYi) almost all target security properties (jailbreaks, prompt injection) with an attack-success oracle; correctness-oriented automated testing of LLMs relies on metamorphic relations (METAL, LLMORPH, Drowzee). We found no work that combines grammar-based input generation with differential (cross-model) oracles on everyday deterministic tasks (arithmetic, counting, reversal, JSON, echo), which is the gap our study fills.

### Inferences
- The LLM-fuzzing literature uses an attack-success oracle (jailbreak/injection); a differential oracle across multiple chat models on tasks with computable ground truth appears unexplored in the sources found, which is a defensible novelty claim (stated as "to our knowledge").
- TitanFuzz's differential testing across DL backends is the best precedent for our cross-model differential oracle.

### Gaps
- I did not verify a final peer-reviewed venue for GPTFuzzer, PromptFuzz or the HouYi paper; cite them as arXiv preprints unless the coordinator confirms a venue.
- I found no paper on grammar-based fuzzing of Hugging Face chat models specifically, nor any on differential testing between chat models on deterministic tasks (searches on arXiv titles for "differential testing" + LLM returned only LLM-assisted differential testing of other software).

## 2. Instruction-following and format-compliance evaluation ([FMT])

### Takeaway
IFEval established "verifiable instructions" checkable by code, the same oracle style we use for JSON/format checks; later benchmarks (FollowBench, InFoBench, FOFO, StructuredRAG, JSONSchemaBench) show format adherence is far from solved, especially in open models, and that format constraints can reduce reasoning accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- Zhou, J., Lu, T., Mishra, S., Brahma, S., Basu, S., Luan, Y., Zhou, D., Hou, L. "Instruction-Following Evaluation for Large Language Models" (IFEval). arXiv preprint, 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07911 — Uses "verifiable instructions" (e.g., "write in more than 400 words"), 25 types identified; relevance: direct precedent for programmatic oracles on format instructions, which our JSON category reuses. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07911)
- Jiang, Y., Wang, Y., Zeng, X., Zhong, W., et al. (10 authors). "FollowBench: A Multi-level Fine-grained Constraints Following Benchmark for Large Language Models." ACL 2024 (Long). DOI: 10.18653/v1/2024.acl-long.257. https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20410 — Relevance: incremental constraint levels, analogous to grammar productions adding constraints to a prompt. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20410); [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.18653/v1/2024.acl-long.257)
- Qin, Y., Song, K., Hu, Y., Yao, W., et al. (10 authors). "InFoBench: Evaluating Instruction Following Ability in Large Language Models." arXiv preprint, 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03601 — Relevance: decomposes instructions into checkable requirements; alternative instruction-following metric. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03601)
- Xia, C., Xing, C., Du, J., Yang, X., Feng, Y., Xu, R., Yin, W., Xiong, C. "FOFO: A Benchmark to Evaluate LLMs' Format-Following Capability." arXiv preprint, 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18667 — Finds open-source models "significantly lag behind closed-source ones in format adherence"; relevance: predicts the JSON violations we observe in small open chatbots. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18667)
- Shorten, C., Pierse, C., Smith, T. B., Cardenas, E., Sharma, A., Trengrove, J., van Luijt, B. "StructuredRAG: JSON Response Formatting with Large Language Models." arXiv preprint, 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11061 — Six JSON-format tasks; average success 82.55% across 24 experiments with Gemini 1.5 Pro and Llama 3 8B-instruct; relevance: quantitative baseline for JSON compliance failures. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11061)

- Sclar, M., Choi, Y., Tsvetkov, Y., Suhr, A. "Quantifying Language Models' Sensitivity to Spurious Features in Prompt Design or: How I learned to start worrying about prompt formatting." ICLR 2024 (author comment). https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11324 — Up to 76 accuracy points of difference from meaning-preserving formatting changes (LLaMA-2-13B); relevance: justifies grammar-based variation of surface form as a way to expose instability. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11324)

### Inferences
- Our JSON/format category can be framed as an IFEval-style verifiable-instruction oracle applied under fuzzed prompt variation, combining IFEval with the Sclar et al. sensitivity finding.

### Gaps
- Final venues for IFEval, InFoBench, FOFO, StructuredRAG, JSONSchemaBench and "Let Me Speak Freely?" were not confirmed; cite as arXiv unless confirmed elsewhere.

## 3. Character-level / tokenization failures ([COUNT], [REV], [ECHO]), arithmetic failures ([ARITH]) and small open models

### Takeaway
There is a substantial 2023-2025 literature attributing letter counting, spelling and character manipulation failures to subword tokenization (CUTE, "strawberry" papers, Shin & Kaneko, Chai et al.), and linking arithmetic errors to number tokenization and to limits of compositional reasoning. Glitch/under-trained tokens and invisible Unicode give a mechanism for verbatim-echo failures on Unicode text. Technical reports document the small open models under test.

### Inferences
- Our counting, reversal and echo categories map cleanly onto the "curse of tokenization" literature; arithmetic maps onto number-tokenization and compositionality work. The Related Work can argue that these are known capability limits, and our contribution is a systematic, automated, differential way to surface and quantify them across deployed chatbots.
- The Strawberry Problem's finding that character skills emerge late in training gives a hypothesis for why smaller models fail more, testable with our data.

### Gaps
- I did not find a dedicated benchmark paper for string reversal alone; reversal is covered within CUTE and character-manipulation papers.
- Technical reports do not, in the abstracts retrieved, report letter-counting or reversal performance for Qwen2.5/SmolLM2/TinyLlama.
- "Reversal curse" (Berglund et al.) concerns reversing relations (A is B -> B is A), not string reversal; it was deliberately not included to avoid confusion.

## 4. Holistic evaluation frameworks and robustness benchmarks

### Takeaway
HELM, BIG-bench and lm-evaluation-harness provide static, curated evaluation; robustness benchmarks (PromptRobust, DecodingTrust) perturb prompts adversarially. Our approach differs by generating inputs from a grammar and judging them with differential/ground-truth oracles, closer to software testing than to leaderboard evaluation.

- Liang, P., Bommasani, R., Lee, T., Tsipras, D., et al. (50 authors). "Holistic Evaluation of Language Models" (HELM). Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR), 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09110 — Taxonomy of scenarios and 7 metrics including robustness; relevance: reference framework for multi-metric evaluation, contrasted with our generated (non-fixed) test inputs. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09110)
