## Classic and foundational work (McKeeman 1998, Csmith, EMI)

### Takeaway
Differential testing started as a way to test several implementations of one specification (C compilers) without a hand-written oracle. Csmith and EMI made it the standard method in compiler testing. Our study carries the same idea over from compilers to chat LLMs. The main difference is that the LLMs do not share a formal specification, so a disagreement is only a signal of failure, and ground truth is needed to decide which model is wrong.

- **Yang, X., Chen, Y., Eide, E., Regehr, J. (2011). It is the metamorphic counterpart to our cross-model setup and a natural complementary oracle, for example paraphrased prompts sent to the same chatbot. — [Crossref/ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/2594291.2594334)
- **Gulzar, M. A., Zhu, Y., Han, X. (2019). "Perception and Practices of Differential Testing." *ICSE-SEIP 2019* (41st Int. It is useful for framing the practical problems we also face, such as triaging the many disagreements that are not real failures. — [Crossref/IEEE DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/icse-seip.2019.00016)
- **Brubaker, C., Jana, S., Ray, B., Khurshid, S., Shmatikov, V. (2014). "Using Frankencerts for Automated Adversarial Testing of Certificate Validation in SSL/TLS Implementations." *2014 IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy (S&P)*.** DOI: 10.1109/SP.2014.15. *Relevance:* a standard example of differential testing across independent implementations from different vendors: recombined certificates are sent to several TLS libraries and their disagreements are flagged. This mirrors our use of several independently trained LLMs. — [Crossref/IEEE DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp.2014.15)
- **Chen, Y., Su, Z. (2015). "Guided differential testing of certificate validation in SSL/TLS implementations." *Proc. 10th Joint Meeting on Foundations of Software Engineering (ESEC/FSE 2015)*.** DOI: 10.1145/2786805.2786835. *Relevance:* shows that guiding the input generator, rather than generating at random, makes differential testing find more discrepancies. - **Chen, Y., Su, T., Sun, C., Su, Z., Zhao, J. (2016). "Coverage-directed differential testing of JVM implementations." *PLDI 2016*; *ACM SIGPLAN Notices* 51(6).** DOI: 10.1145/2980983.2908095. *Relevance:* combines coverage feedback with a differential oracle across JVMs. This parallels combining a fuzzer's exploration signal with cross-model disagreement. — [Crossref/ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/2980983.2908095)

### Inferences
- In the classic setting, any disagreement proves that at least one implementation is wrong, because all of them implement the same specification. LLMs have no shared specification and can legitimately differ, especially on open-ended prompts. The Related Work section should therefore present our use of ground truth to decide which model is wrong as the departure from McKeeman, Csmith and EMI, not as an incidental detail.
- Csmith's main lesson, that the generator must avoid undefined behavior or the differential signal is dominated by false positives, carries over directly: grammar-generated prompts should have one determinate correct answer wherever ground truth is claimed.

## Differential testing of deep-learning systems and libraries

### Takeaway
Differential testing moved into ML in two strands. The first compares several trained models on the same input: DeepXplore is the closest ancestor to our cross-model oracle. The second compares DL library backends or APIs: CRADLE, LEMON, Audee, FreeFuzz, DeepREL, NNSmith. More recently, LLMs have been used as the input generator (TitanFuzz, FuzzGPT, Fuzz4All, DLLens), with differential checking as the oracle.

### Cited Findings
- **Pei, K., Cao, Y., Yang, J., Jana, S. (2017). "DeepXplore: Automated Whitebox Testing of Deep Learning Systems." *Proc. 26th ACM Symposium on Operating Systems Principles (SOSP '17)*.** DOI: 10.1145/3132747.3132785. A research highlight appeared in *Communications of the ACM* (2019), DOI 10.1145/3361566. *Relevance:* DeepXplore is the most direct precedent for our design. It feeds the same input to several DNNs trained for the same task and treats their disagreement as a sign of erroneous behavior, with no manual labels. We do the same with chat LLMs, and add ground truth to assign blame. — [Crossref/ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3132747.3132785)
- **Pham, H. V., Lutellier, T., Qi, W., Tan, L. (2019). "CRADLE: Cross-Backend Validation to Detect and Localize Bugs in Deep Learning Libraries." *41st IEEE/ACM Int. Conf. on Software Engineering (ICSE 2019)*.** DOI: 10.1109/ICSE.2019.00107. *Relevance:* runs the same model on different Keras backends and uses distance metrics to separate real inconsistencies from numeric noise. This is analogous to our need for a similarity threshold to decide when two chatbot answers really disagree. — [Crossref/IEEE DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/icse.2019.00107)
- **Wang, Z., Yan, M., Chen, J., Liu, S., Zhang, D. (2020). "Deep learning library testing via effective model generation" (LEMON). *ESEC/FSE 2020*.** DOI: 10.1145/3368089.3409761. *Relevance:* mutates models to make differential testing across DL libraries more effective. This is a precedent for generating inputs specifically to increase the chance of disagreement. — [Crossref/ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3368089.3409761)
- **Guo, Q., Xie, X., Li, Y., Zhang, X., Liu, Y., Li, X., Shen, C. (2020). "Audee: Automated Testing for Deep Learning Frameworks." *35th IEEE/ACM Int. Conf. on Automated Software Engineering (ASE 2020)*.** DOI: 10.1145/3324884.3416571. *Relevance:* differential testing across DL frameworks combined with search-based input generation. It is another example of pairing a generator with a cross-implementation oracle. — [Crossref/ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3324884.3416571)
- **Wei, A., Deng, Y., Yang, C., Zhang, L. (2022). "Free Lunch for Testing: Fuzzing Deep-Learning Libraries from Open Source" (FreeFuzz). *44th Int. Conf. on Software Engineering (ICSE 2022)*.** DOI: 10.1145/3510003.3510041. *Relevance:* builds its fuzzing inputs from real API usage it mines and checks results differentially across CPU and GPU backends. This supports building input generators from real-world data, as a grammar can be built from real user prompts. — [Crossref/ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3510003.3510041)
- **Deng, Y., Yang, C., Wei, A., Zhang, L. (2022). "Fuzzing deep-learning libraries via automated relational API inference" (DeepREL). *ESEC/FSE 2022*.** DOI: 10.1145/3540250.3549085. *Relevance:* uses inferred relations between APIs that should be equivalent as a differential oracle when no second implementation exists. This is relevant because chatbots with nominally equal capability are only loosely equivalent "implementations". — [Crossref/ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3540250.3549085)
- **Liu, J., Lin, J., Ruffy, F., Tan, C., Li, J., Panda, A., Zhang, L. (2023). "NNSmith: Generating Diverse and Valid Test Cases for Deep Learning Compilers." *ASPLOS 2023, Vol. 2*.** DOI: 10.1145/3575693.3575707. *Relevance:* a Csmith-style generator of valid models for differential testing of DL compilers. It supports our argument that generating valid, grammar-constrained inputs is what makes a differential oracle usable. — [Crossref/ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3575693.3575707)
- **Deng, Y., Xia, C. S., Peng, H., Yang, C., Zhang, L. (2023). "Large Language Models Are Zero-Shot Fuzzers: Fuzzing Deep-Learning Libraries via Large Language Models" (TitanFuzz). *32nd ACM SIGSOFT Int. Symp. on Software Testing and Analysis (ISSTA 2023)*.** DOI: 10.1145/3597926.3598067. *Relevance:* uses an LLM to generate DL API programs and checks them with a differential CPU/GPU oracle. Here the LLM is the generator, whereas in our study the LLM is the system under test. — [Crossref/ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3597926.3598067)
- **Deng, Y., Xia, C. S., Yang, C., Zhang, S. D., Yang, S., Zhang, L. (2024). "Large Language Models are Edge-Case Generators: Crafting Unusual Programs for Fuzzing Deep Learning Libraries" (FuzzGPT). *IEEE/ACM 46th Int. Conf. on Software Engineering (ICSE 2024)*.** DOI: 10.1145/3597503.3623343. *Relevance:* steers LLM generation toward unusual edge cases. This is analogous to biasing our grammar toward rare constructions, which are the ones most likely to make chatbots disagree. — [Crossref/ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3597503.3623343)
- **Li, M., Li, D., Liu, J., Cao, J., Tian, Y., Cheung, S.-C. (2026, online 2025). "Enhancing Differential Testing with LLMs for Testing Deep Learning Libraries" (DLLens). *ACM Transactions on Software Engineering and Methodology (TOSEM)*.** DOI: 10.1145/3735637 (arXiv 2406.07944). Crossref lists the year as 2026. *Relevance:* uses an LLM to find counterpart implementations that serve as the differential reference. This addresses the "no second implementation" problem that our use of several models avoids. — [Crossref/ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3735637); [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07944)

### Inferences
- DeepXplore is the closest prior work, and the paper should cite it explicitly as the source of "cross-model disagreement as an oracle". Our contributions over it are: (a) the domain (free-form text from chat LLMs instead of classifiers or steering angles), (b) a grammar-based generator instead of gradient-guided perturbation, and (c) ground truth to break ties.
- CRADLE's distance-based inconsistency metric is a useful precedent for how we define "disagreement" between textual answers (exact match, normalized answer, or semantic similarity threshold).

### Gaps
- Not checked in this pass, but possibly relevant: Muffin (ICSE 2022, DOI 10.1145/3510003.3510092 appeared in Crossref results but I did not inspect it) and DocTer (ISSTA 2022, DOI 10.1145/3533767.3534220, verified to exist; it is mainly documentation-guided fuzzing rather than differential testing).

## Differential testing applied to LLMs or across LLM models

### Takeaway
Most "LLM + differential testing" work uses LLMs as test generators for other software: Fuzz4All, Differential Prompting, Mokav, DiffSpec, LLMeDiff, OpDiffer. Work that tests the LLMs themselves mostly uses metamorphic relations within one model (METAL, LLMORPH, the ICSME 2025 study), or comes from the NLP hallucination literature, where cross-model consistency (SAC3, Finch-Zk, LM vs LM) serves as a black-box error signal. I found no peer-reviewed software-testing paper that does exactly what we do (grammar-based fuzzing plus a cross-model differential oracle over open chat LLMs, with ground truth for blame), which leaves room for our contribution. Absence of evidence is not proof, though.

### Inferences
- The hallucination literature (SAC3, Finch-Zk, LM vs LM, SelfCheckGPT, multiagent debate) and the software-testing literature (DeepXplore, metamorphic LLM testing) rarely cite each other. A Related Work section that connects the two strands under "differential oracle" would be a contribution in itself.
- SAC3's argument that models can be confidently and consistently wrong, together with the general caveat that LLMs may agree on wrong answers, implies that cross-model agreement does not prove correctness. This justifies our use of ground truth, and suggests we should also report "consensus-wrong" cases (all models agree but ground truth disagrees) as failures that a purely differential oracle cannot see.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed paper that applies grammar-based fuzzing with a cross-model differential oracle specifically to open chat LLMs on Hugging Face. One web search summary reached the same conclusion, but that is not exhaustive. Worth a final check on ACM DL or IEEE Xplore, which I could not query directly.
- I did not verify the venues of DiffSpec, LLMeDiff, Finch-Zk or multiagent debate; treat them as preprints unless confirmed.
- Not checked for lack of time: HELM (Liang et al., TMLR 2023) as a cross-model benchmarking reference, and differential testing of LLM code generation (e.g., LLMCodeChoice, ISSTA 2024, mentioned in a search summary but not opened).
