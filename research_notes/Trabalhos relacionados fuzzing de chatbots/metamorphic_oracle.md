## Oracle problem and metamorphic testing foundations

### Takeaway
The test-oracle problem means it is hard to decide whether an output is correct. It is the canonical framing for testing LLMs, and metamorphic testing (MT) is the standard, well-surveyed way to reduce it. Our study combines two oracle types that Barr et al. classify separately: a metamorphic oracle (paraphrase invariance) and a specified or derived ground-truth oracle (verifiable tasks).

### Cited Findings
- **Weyuker, E. J.** "On Testing Non-Testable Programs." *The Computer Journal* 25(4):465–470, 1982. DOI: 10.1093/comjnl/25.4.465. This classic introduced "non-testable programs", meaning programs for which no oracle exists. A chatbot answering free-form requests fits the definition, which is why we fall back on verifiable tasks. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/comjnl/25.4.465)
- **Davis, M. D.; Weyuker, E. J.** "Pseudo-oracles for non-testable programs." *Proceedings of the ACM '81 Conference*, 1981. DOI: 10.1145/800175.809889. This paper started the pseudo-oracle (N-version or differential) idea, the conceptual ancestor of the "differential" part of our fuzzing. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/800175.809889)
- **Chen, T. Y.; Cheung, S. C.; Yiu, S. M.** "Metamorphic Testing: A New Approach for Generating Next Test Cases." Technical Report HKUST-CS98-01, Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, 1998 (posted as arXiv:2002.12543, 2020). The original MT proposal derives follow-up test cases from successful ones. It explicitly cites Weyuker's point that oracles are "pragmatically unattainable in most situations", and this is the origin of our source-prompt/paraphrase follow-up design. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12543)
- **Barr, E. T.; Harman, M.; McMinn, P.; Shahbaz, M.; Yoo, S.** "The Oracle Problem in Software Testing: A Survey." *IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering* 41(5):507–525, 2015. DOI: 10.1109/TSE.2014.2372785. This is the reference survey of oracle automation (specified, derived and implicit oracles, metamorphic testing, the human oracle). We use it to classify our two oracles: paraphrase-MR as a derived/metamorphic oracle and verifiable tasks as specified oracles. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.2014.2372785); abstract via [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1109/tse.2014.2372785)
- **Segura, S.; Fraser, G.; Sanchez, A. B.; Ruiz-Cortés, A.** "A Survey on Metamorphic Testing." *IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering* 42(9):805–824, 2016. DOI: 10.1109/TSE.2016.2532875. This literature survey of MT (MR identification, application domains, open problems) justifies MT as a mature technique to apply to chatbots. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.2016.2532875)
- **Chen, T. Y.; Kuo, F.-C.; Liu, H.; Poon, P.-L.; Towey, D.; Tse, T. H.; Zhou, Z. Q.** "Metamorphic Testing: A Review of Challenges and Opportunities." *ACM Computing Surveys* 51(1), Article 4, pp. 1–27, 2018. DOI: 10.1145/3143561. This review by MT's originators covers challenges such as MR generation and MR effectiveness. We cite it as the authoritative definition of an MR. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3143561) (note: the Crossref record shows only the short title "Metamorphic Testing", with authors and the volume, issue and pages above. The subtitle comes from the published article and could not be re-checked because dl.acm.org was blocked.)
- **Zhang, J. M.; Harman, M.; Ma, L.; Liu, Y.** "Machine Learning Testing: Survey, Landscapes and Horizons." *IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering* 48(1):1–36, 2022 (online 2019). DOI: 10.1109/TSE.2019.2962027. This is the standard ML-testing survey, which names the oracle problem and MT as central to ML testing. It is the bridge from classic MT to testing learned models. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.2019.2962027) (Crossref verified the title, authors, venue and year. The volume and pages come from my own knowledge and should be checked.)
- **Zhang, J.; Sun, C.-a.; Liu, H.; Dong, S.** "Can Large Language Models Discover Metamorphic Relations? A Large-Scale Empirical Study." *IEEE SANER 2025*, pp. 24–35. DOI: 10.1109/SANER64311.2025.00011. This work turns the relationship around, using LLMs to identify MRs across 37 systems under test. It is a useful contrast, since we use a hand-chosen MR (paraphrase invariance) to test the LLM itself. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/SANER64311.2025.00011); abstract via [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1109/saner64311.2025.00011)

### Inferences
- Barr et al.'s taxonomy lets us say precisely that our design pairs a metamorphic oracle (no ground truth needed, so it scales to any prompt) with specified oracles on a restricted, verifiable task subset. This addresses MT's known weakness: an MR violation shows inconsistency, but not which answer is wrong.

## Behavioral and metamorphic testing of NLP models (pre-LLM)

### Takeaway
Before LLMs, SE and NLP research had already established perturbation-based metamorphic testing of NLP systems: invariance tests (CheckList), machine-translation MT (SIT, TransRepair, PatInv, RTI), QA-software MT (QAAskeR, QAQA), fairness MT, and paraphrase-consistency benchmarks (ParaRel, BECEL). Our paraphrase-invariance MR is a direct descendant of CheckList's INV tests and the MT-for-MT line of work.

### Cited Findings
- **Ribeiro, M. T.; Wu, T.; Guestrin, C.; Singh, S.** "Beyond Accuracy: Behavioral Testing of NLP Models with CheckList." *Proceedings of the 58th Annual Meeting of the ACL (ACL 2020)*, pp. 4902–4912. DOI: 10.18653/v1/2020.acl-main.442 (Best Paper; extended abstract also at IJCAI 2021, DOI 10.24963/ijcai.2021/659). CheckList proposes software-engineering-style behavioral testing with Minimum Functionality, Invariance (INV) and Directional tests. In a user study, practitioners using CheckList "created twice as many tests, and found almost three times as many bugs". Our paraphrase MR is an INV test applied to chat LLMs. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2020.acl-main.442); abstract via [arXiv:2005.04118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04118)
- **Zheng, W.; Wang, W.; Liu, D.; Zhang, C.; Zeng, Q.; Deng, Y.; Yang, W.; He, P.; Xie, T.** "Testing Untestable Neural Machine Translation: An Industrial Case." *ICSE 2019 Companion*, 2019. DOI: 10.1109/ICSE-Companion.2019.00131. This early industrial (WeChat/Tencent) MT-style testing of NMT explicitly applies Weyuker's "untestable" framing to neural NLP. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICSE-Companion.2019.00131)
- **He, P.; Meister, C.; Su, Z.** "Structure-Invariant Testing for Machine Translation." *ICSE 2020*, pp. 961–973. DOI: 10.1145/3377811.3380339. SIT is a metamorphic approach: the translations of similar source sentences (one word substituted) should have similar syntax-tree structure. This is the same "similar input should give similar output" logic we apply to paraphrased chat prompts. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3377811.3380339); abstract via [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1145/3377811.3380339)
- **Sun, Z.; Zhang, J. M.; Harman, M.; Papadakis, M.; Zhang, L.** "Automatic Testing and Improvement of Machine Translation." *ICSE 2020*, pp. 974–985. DOI: 10.1145/3377811.3380420. TransRepair combines mutation with metamorphic testing and finds roughly 36% (Google Translate) and 40% (Transformer) inconsistency bugs "without access to human oracles". It is a strong precedent for consistency bugs as a test target and a source of headline numbers. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3377811.3380420); abstract via [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1145/3377811.3380420)
- **Gupta, S.; He, P.; Meister, C.; Su, Z.** "Machine Translation Testing via Pathological Invariance." *ESEC/FSE 2020*, pp. 863–875. DOI: 10.1145/3368089.3409756. PatInv is the complementary MR: sentences with different meanings must not translate identically. It is useful for arguing that paraphrase invariance should be paired with a check that the model does not collapse distinct requests. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3368089.3409756)
- **He, P.; Meister, C.; Su, Z.** "Testing Machine Translation via Referential Transparency." *ICSE 2021*, pp. 410–422. DOI: 10.1109/ICSE43902.2021.00047. RTI is MT-based on the idea that a referentially transparent phrase should translate the same way in different contexts. It is another invariance MR on NLP output. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICSE43902.2021.00047)
- **Chen, S.; Jin, S.; Xie, X.** "Testing Your Question Answering Software via Asking Recursively." *ASE 2021*. DOI: 10.1109/ASE51524.2021.9678670. QAAskeR is metamorphic testing of QA software that re-asks questions built from earlier answers. It is the closest pre-LLM analogue to testing a question-answering chatbot with MRs. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ASE51524.2021.9678670)
- **Shen, Q.; Chen, J.; Zhang, J. M.; Wang, H.; Liu, S.; Tian, M.** "Natural Test Generation for Precise Testing of Question Answering Software." *ASE 2022*. DOI: 10.1145/3551349.3556953. QAQA generates more natural MT test inputs for QA to reduce false positives. Its concern with the naturalness and validity of generated follow-ups mirrors our concern that grammar-generated paraphrases really preserve meaning. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3551349.3556953)
- **Ma, P.; Wang, S.; Liu, J.** "Metamorphic Testing and Certified Mitigation of Fairness Violations in NLP Models." *IJCAI 2020*. DOI: 10.24963/ijcai.2020/64. This work applies MT to NLP fairness (protected-attribute substitution MRs) and shows that MRs on text inputs generalize beyond translation. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.24963/ijcai.2020/64)
- **Elazar, Y.; Kassner, N.; Ravfogel, S.; Ravichander, A.; Hovy, E.; Schütze, H.; Goldberg, Y.** "Measuring and Improving Consistency in Pretrained Language Models." *Transactions of the ACL* 9, 2021. DOI: 10.1162/tacl_a_00410. ParaRel provides 328 paraphrases of 38 factual relations. It defines consistency as "the invariance of its behavior under meaning-preserving alternations in its input" and finds the consistency of all tested PLMs "poor". This is essentially our MR, measured on masked LMs. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/tacl_a_00410); abstract via [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1162/tacl_a_00410)
- **Jang, M.; Kwon, D. S.; Lukasiewicz, T.** "BECEL: Benchmark for Consistency Evaluation of Language Models." *COLING 2022*, pp. 3680–3696. ACL Anthology ID 2022.coling-1.324. This benchmark has 19 test cases over several consistency types (including semantic-equivalence consistency). It reports that no PLM does well on every test case and that larger models are not reliably more consistent. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2022.coling-1.324/) (verified via search result and anthology listing; direct page fetch blocked)
- **Henkel, J.; Ramakrishnan, G.; Wang, Z.; Albarghouthi, A.; Jha, S.; Reps, T.** "Semantic Robustness of Models of Source Code." *IEEE SANER 2022*. DOI: 10.1109/SANER53432.2022.00070. This work studies robustness of code models to semantics-preserving program transformations, the code-domain counterpart of meaning-preserving paraphrase. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/SANER53432.2022.00070)

### Inferences
- The MT-for-MT literature (SIT, TransRepair, PatInv, RTI) is the strongest SE-venue precedent for treating output inconsistency under meaning-preserving input change as a bug without a human oracle. Our work carries this from translators to general chat LLMs, with a grammar as the follow-up generator instead of word substitution.
- QAQA's naturalness concern is a likely reviewer question for grammar-generated paraphrases, so cite it when discussing threats to validity.

### Gaps
- I did not find a specific peer-reviewed pre-LLM paper on "paraphrase robustness" of chatbots or dialogue systems. ParaRel and BECEL are the closest consistency benchmarks.

## Metamorphic testing, consistency and paraphrase/prompt robustness of LLMs (2022–2026)

### Takeaway
From 2023 to 2026 a sizeable body of work shows that LLMs are highly sensitive to meaning-preserving prompt changes. Format changes alone can swing accuracy by up to 76 points (Sclar et al.), and single-prompt benchmarks are brittle (Mizrahi et al.). SE venues now host dedicated LLM metamorphic-testing frameworks (METAL at ICST 2024, Drowzee at OOPSLA/PACMPL 2024, LLMorph and the 191-MR study at ASE/ICSME 2025, MORTAR at TSE 2026). Self-consistency (Wang et al.) and generator-validator consistency (Li et al.) represent the "consistency as signal" view. Our contribution differs by pairing paraphrase MRs with verifiable-task ground truth on open Hugging Face chat models.

### Cited Findings
**LLM metamorphic-testing frameworks (SE venues)**

Conf. on Software Testing, Verification and Validation). DOI: 10.1109/ICST60714.2024.00019. METAL generates hundreds of MRs from templates covering quality attributes (robustness, fairness and others) across tasks, and adds MR-effectiveness metrics beyond attack success rate. It is the closest framework-level precedent for our tool. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICST60714.2024.00019); abstract via [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1109/icst60714.2024.00019) (Semantic Scholar mislabels the venue. - **Cho, S.; Ruberto, S.; Terragni, V.** "Metamorphic Testing of Large Language Models for Natural Language Processing." *IEEE ICSME 2025*. DOI: 10.1109/ICSME64153.2025.00025. The authors call it "the most comprehensive study of MT for LLMs to date". They collected 191 MRs for NLP tasks from the literature and ran about 560K metamorphic tests (36 MRs, 3 LLMs). Use it as the catalogue against which to position our paraphrase MR. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICSME64153.2025.00025); abstract via [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1109/icsme64153.2025.00025)
- **Cho, S.; Ruberto, S.; Terragni, V.** "LLMorph: Automated Metamorphic Testing of Large Language Models." *ASE 2025 (tool track)*. DOI: 10.1109/ASE63991.2025.00385. This is the tool behind the study above: 36 MRs, 4 NLP benchmarks, GPT-4, LLaMA3 and Hermes 2, over 561,000 test executions, with open-source code. It is a direct comparison point and a possible baseline. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ASE63991.2025.00385); abstract via [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1109/ase63991.2025.00385)
- **Guo, G.; Aleti, A.; Neelofar, N.; Tantithamthavorn, C.; Qi, Y.; Chen, T. Y.** "MORTAR: Multi-Turn Metamorphic Testing for LLM-Based Dialogue Systems." *IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering*, 2026. DOI: 10.1109/TSE.2026.3701230. This is metamorphic testing of LLM dialogue systems with dialogue-level perturbations and automated MR matching. Across 8 systems it reveals 51% more bugs per test case than the best single-turn MT baseline, with 24.6% higher precision. It is the closest work on *chatbots* specifically, though multi-turn while ours is single-turn paraphrase. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.2026.3701230); abstract via [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1109/tse.2026.3701230) (arXiv preprint dated 2024; journal version 2026)
- **Li, N.; Li, Y.; Liu, Y.; Shi, L.; Wang, K.; Wang, H.** "Drowzee: Metamorphic Testing for Fact-Conflicting Hallucination Detection in Large Language Models." *Proceedings of the ACM on Programming Languages* 8 (OOPSLA2), 2024. DOI: 10.1145/3689776. Drowzee uses logic-programming rules to derive test cases *with ground-truth answers* from a knowledge base and adds semantic-aware oracles. It is the closest precedent for combining MT with ground-truth oracles, which is our verifiable-task design. — [Crossref/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3689776); abstract via [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1145/3689776)

**Prompt-format and paraphrase sensitivity (NLP/ML venues)**
- **Sclar, M.; Choi, Y.; Tsvetkov, Y.; Suhr, A.** "Quantifying Language Models' Sensitivity to Spurious Features in Prompt Design or: How I Learned to Start Worrying about Prompt Formatting." *ICLR 2024*. arXiv:2310.11324. Open-source LLMs are "extremely sensitive to subtle changes in prompt formatting", with "performance differences of up to 76 accuracy points" (LLaMA-2-13B). **Self-consistency and internal consistency**
- **Wang, X.; Wei, J.; Schuurmans, D.; Le, Q.; Chi, E.; Narang, S.; Chowdhery, A.; Zhou, D.** "Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning in Language Models." *ICLR 2023*. arXiv:2203.11171. GPT-4 (0613) is GV-consistent only 76% of the time, and consistency fine-tuning raises Alpaca-30B from about 60% to 93%. It is another LLM self-consistency MR. — [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/bfcb583d225b1db8d3ca2331f6785774-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01846) (the numbers are from the search-result summary of the abstract)

### Inferences
- Position our work as follows. (1) The MR is classic MT/CheckList-INV paraphrase invariance. (2) The novelty is grammar-based generation of paraphrases plus *differential* comparison across Hugging Face chat models. (3) Verifiable tasks (arithmetic, letter counting, reversal, JSON) give ground truth, which resolves the MT limitation that an MR violation does not say which output is wrong. Drowzee and ReCode are the nearest precedents for MT combined with ground truth. LLMorph, METAL and MORTAR are the nearest LLM MT frameworks.
- Sclar et al. and Mizrahi et al. supply the quantitative motivation: prompt variation alone can move accuracy by tens of points, so single-phrasing evaluation of chatbots is unreliable.
- Self-consistency (Wang et al.) should be cited to *distinguish* our work. Its consistency is over sampled outputs for one input, and it is used to improve accuracy, not to find bugs.
