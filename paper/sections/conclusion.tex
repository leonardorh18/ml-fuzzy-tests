\section{Conclusion and Future Work}\label{sec:conclusion}

We combined grammar-based, coverage-guided fuzzing with differential testing across models and metamorphic testing across paraphrases, and evaluated the combination on three small open chat models from the Hugging Face Hub. Because every grammar derivation carries its expected answer, we could measure not only how many failures the approach reveals but also how reliable its oracles are. The approach revealed failures in 64.5\% of the graded replies, concentrated on character-level tasks, large operands, output-format instructions, and Unicode payloads, and exposed answers that change with wording alone. Without ground truth, the differential oracle would have flagged almost all failures but could not have said which model was wrong, while the metamorphic oracle was more precise and missed consistently wrong answers. Coverage guidance reached full grammar coverage far more reliably than random expansion. The main limitation we found lies in the oracles rather than in input generation: their definition of a correct reply changed one model's result in one category from 0\% to 83\%.

Future work includes running the campaign against hosted models through the Hugging Face API, with sampling and repeated queries to separate non-determinism from inconsistency; testing larger and more similar models, where shared wrong answers should be more frequent; extending the grammar with multi-turn dialogues and with tasks whose answers can be checked by a program, such as code or unit conversions; using an independent human rater for the oracle validation; and measuring behavioral coverage of the model, not only coverage of the grammar.
