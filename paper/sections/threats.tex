\section{Threats to Validity and Limitations}\label{sec:threats}

\textbf{Construct validity.} The ground-truth oracles rely on heuristics: the numeric oracle takes the last integer in the reply, the reversal oracle strips quotes and a final period, the echo oracle accepts the payload anywhere in the reply, and the JSON oracle rejects objects embedded in prose. RQ4 shows that these choices cause systematic errors, and the JSON choice alone changes one model's pass rate in one category from 0\% to 83\%; we report the lenient variant alongside the strict one. The differential oracle compares canonical answers, so two equivalent replies with different surface forms may count as a disagreement, and the precision we report for it depends on the canonicalization. The \code{edge} category only checks for runtime errors and empty replies; our classification of its replies is manual and coarse.

\textbf{Internal validity.} Greedy decoding makes each local model deterministic for a given software stack, and repeated prompts received identical replies, but results may change with other library versions, hardware, or chat templates. Replies cut at the 256-token limit are flagged and reported (37 of 510, 10 of them graded failures), not discarded; a larger limit could turn some of these failures into passes. The manual judgments for RQ4 and the \code{edge} labels were made with the assistance of an AI model and have not yet been independently checked by the authors; a second human rater would allow an inter-rater agreement measure. The sample of 60 replies gives a wide confidence interval (86--98\% agreement).

\textbf{External validity.} We tested three small models (360\,M to 1.1\,B parameters) running locally, not the hosted Hugging Face API, which we implemented but could not use without an access token. Larger models, hosted deployments with sampling, and other providers may behave very differently; in particular, stronger models would likely fail less often and share more errors, which would lower the recall of the differential oracle. The prompts are in English, single-turn, and cover six task categories; they do not represent open-ended conversation, and multi-turn effects \citep{guo2026mortar} are outside our scope.

\textbf{Conclusion validity.} The main campaign uses one grammar seed and ten skeletons per category, so per-category rates have wide intervals (Table~\ref{tab:passrates}), and some sub-cases, such as letters absent from the word, were not drawn at all. Ablation~2 uses three seeds, so its differences are indicative rather than statistically established. Ablation~1, which needs no models, uses 30 seeds.

\textbf{Naturalness of generated prompts.} Grammar-derived prompts are well formed but templated, and the \code{echo} grammar admits only two wordings. Real users phrase requests more freely, so our metamorphic results bound paraphrase sensitivity from below for the wordings we wrote, not for natural variation in general.
