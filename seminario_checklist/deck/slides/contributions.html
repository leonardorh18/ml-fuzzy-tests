Ribeiro et al. (2020), §1 and §6 · github.com/marcotcr/checklist

Part 1: So what does the paper propose? Three things. First, a method: test language models the way we test software, checking one skill at a time, such as negation or names, instead of looking at one overall score. It works for any task and any model. Second, a free, open-source tool that generates thousands of test cases automatically, so this kind of testing is cheap. Third, evidence that it works: with CheckList, the authors found serious bugs both in research models like BERT and in commercial products from Microsoft, Google and Amazon; and people who used CheckList found about three times as many bugs as people who did not.
