Surprise: research models beat commercial ones on most tests.

Ribeiro et al. (2020), Table 1 (rounded; tests selected by the authors)

Here are three sentiment tests, with failure rates rounded; higher means worse. When the negation comes at the end, as in “I thought the plane would be awful, but it wasn’t”, Microsoft and Amazon fail every case and Google fails 90%. A question followed by “No” and a change over time, “I used to hate this airline, now I like it”, also cause many failures. Other tests in the paper show sensitivity to irrelevant changes: adding a random URL changes 25% of Amazon’s predictions, and switching a location changes 21% of Google’s. Two findings stand out. First, research models beat the commercial ones on almost every test, except neutral sentences, which BERT and RoBERTa cannot predict because they were trained only on positive and negative. Second, BERT and RoBERTa are over 90% accurate on SST-2 sentences with negation, yet fail simple negation tests, showing how aggregate accuracy can mislead.
