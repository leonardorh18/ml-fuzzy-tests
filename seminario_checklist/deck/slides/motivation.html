1 · INTRODUCTION

The problem: accuracy hides failures

Biased test sets

Same biases as training data

One number

Does not show where models fail

Narrow alternatives

One task or one capability

Idea: test NLP models the way we test software.

Ribeiro et al. (2020), §1

The standard paradigm in NLP is to split data into training, validation and test sets and report held-out accuracy, often on public leaderboards. The authors identify three problems. First, held-out sets are rarely comprehensive and contain the same biases as the training data, so real-world performance may be overestimated. Second, a single number does not tell us where the model fails or how to fix it. Third, existing alternative evaluations, such as robustness or fairness tests, focus on individual tasks or a few capabilities. Software engineering faced a similar problem and answered it with behavioral, or black-box, testing: checking input–output behavior capability by capability.
