PART 3 · METHODOLOGY

Three test types

MFT

Simple labelled cases

“I didn’t love the flight.” → negative

INV

Change should not matter

Cuba → Canada: same prediction

DIR

Change has a known effect

+ “You are lame.”: not more positive

INV and DIR need no labels.

Ribeiro et al. (2020), §2.2

The columns of the matrix are three test types. A Minimum Functionality Test, or MFT, is like a unit test: simple examples with labels that check one behavior. An Invariance test applies a change that should not alter the label, such as replacing a location, and checks that the prediction stays the same. A Directional Expectation test applies a change with a known effect, such as appending a negative sentence, and checks that the prediction moves only in the allowed direction. INV and DIR are metamorphic tests, after Segura et al. (2016): they compare predictions before and after the change, so they work on unlabeled data.
