Ribeiro et al. (2020), §3, §4.1 and §6

Part 4: Where can CheckList be used in practice? The paper points to four main applications. First, choosing a model: because tests are applied capability by capability, we can compare models in detail instead of by a single score; the authors did exactly this when they compared research models with the commercial services of Microsoft, Google and Amazon. Second, the development cycle: the same test suite can be re-run on every new version of a model; Microsoft’s team said they would incorporate CheckList into their development cycle and fix the bugs they found in the next model iteration. Third, benchmarks and evaluation pipelines: the authors state that tests created with CheckList can be applied to any model and easily incorporated into current benchmarks or evaluation pipelines. Fourth, shared test suites: since many tests transfer across tasks as they are, like typos, or with small changes, like names, the authors expect teams to create test suites collaboratively. And all of this works on any model, even a paid API, because CheckList is a black-box method: it needs no access to the training data or to the model’s weights.
